Generous pricing proposal - May 21, 2026

Drop prices hard, but keep the meter honest.

The branch currently makes customers bring their own Claude or Codex credential. That means Hetchy's billable cost floor is Daytona sandbox runtime plus product overhead, not Anthropic inference. On that model we can make the plans meaningfully cheaper than the first draft while keeping gross margin near the lower side of acceptable when customers fully use their included credits.

$0.050 Live Daytona cost floor for one fully used 15-minute Standard credit.
64 Hetchy-labelled Daytona sandboxes found through the CLI.
$19 Lowest paid plan that still holds roughly 50% modeled gross margin at full burn.

Recommended Move

Replace the current $49 / $199 / $499 ladder with $19 / $79 / $199. Keep Business at $499, but size included credits against the live Daytona resource shape instead of the smaller branch meter.

Boundary

These prices are compute-only. If Hetchy starts bundling Claude API or Managed Agents usage, model inference must be metered separately or passed through.

Control

Keep credits as 15-minute started blocks and launch with two sandbox flavors: Standard at 2 vCPU / 6 GiB / 10 GiB, and Plus at the current Daytona account ceiling of 4 vCPU / 8 GiB / 10 GiB.

Competitive Read

The market is moving away from subsidies

GitHub says Copilot is moving to usage-based billing on June 1, 2026 because agentic use now includes long multi-step sessions and the old premium-request model no longer matched compute and inference cost. That is the clearest public signal that flat-rate coding agents have been subsidizing heavy users.

Hetchy can still be aggressive

Hetchy is not currently absorbing Anthropic token spend. The product can compete on low base price and clear included sandbox credits, then let customers manage their own Claude/Codex plan or API bill.

Margin Benchmark

10-16% is not enough

The previous generous draft stayed positive, but only barely. That is closer to intentional subsidy than a sustainable SaaS margin. It leaves almost nothing for customer support, failed-run cleanup, onboarding, payment disputes, monitoring, or ordinary operating mistakes.

Use 50% as the floor

Traditional SaaS aims much higher: 70-80% is the normal target and 75%+ is commonly considered good. AI and infrastructure-heavy products can run lower, but the lower defensible anchor is still around 50% with a clear path to 60-70% as usage and infrastructure efficiency improve.

Benchmark signal What the sources say Hetchy pricing interpretation
Traditional SaaS Stripe describes 75%+ as good, below 70% as concerning, and 80%+ as top-performing. Do not call a 10-20% product margin healthy just because it is positive.
Early-stage SaaS Stripe says 50%+ can be acceptable early; CFO Pro says many early-stage SaaS companies sit around 50-60% before optimizing. Use 50% as the lower-side floor for launch pricing, not as the long-term target.
AI startups Bessemer reports AI Supernovas around 25% gross margin, often negative, while AI Shooting Stars average around 60%. We should not copy the subsidy-heavy Supernova pattern unless the goal is explicitly to buy distribution with losses.
Infrastructure-heavy SaaS Benchmarks still expect a credible path to 60-70% as scale improves and cloud costs are optimized. Anchor the public plan on about 50% modeled gross margin and push overage pricing above that.

Cost Floor

Direct sandbox cost

Daytona's public prices are $0.0504 per vCPU-hour, $0.0162 per GiB-hour of memory, and $0.000108 per GiB-hour of storage after the first 5 free GiB. Live Hetchy sandboxes are mostly 2 vCPU, 6 GiB memory, and 10 GiB disk, so the direct Standard floor is higher than the first draft assumed.

live_standard_hour = (2 * $0.0504) + (6 * $0.0162) + (10 * $0.000108) = $0.19908 live_standard_credit = live_standard_hour / 4 = $0.04977 billable_credits = ceil(minutes / 15) * flavor_multiplier

Modeled payment overhead

Margin tables below include a conservative Stripe allowance of 3.6% plus $0.30 per transaction: 2.9% domestic card processing plus Stripe Billing pay-as-you-go at 0.7%. If Stripe Billing is on a negotiated package, margins improve.

modeled_fee = (price * 0.036) + $0.30 gross_margin = (price - fee - overhead - daytona_cogs) / price
Update `internal/billing/flavor.go` before shipping these plan constants. Standard should be 2 vCPU / 6 GiB / 10 GiB. The higher launch flavor should be 4 vCPU / 8 GiB / 10 GiB and should cost 2 credits per started 15-minute block. The old 2 / 3 / 4 Standard tuple and the old 8 / 16 / 25 Max tuple do not match current operational reality.

How Much We Can Drop

Relative to the current branch constants, the public entry price can drop from $49 to $19, Team from $199 to $79, and Growth from $499 to $199. The live Daytona shape and margin benchmarks mean included credits should drop materially from the first generous draft, while still giving customers a clear monthly run budget.

Why This Still Works

At the live $0.04977 Standard credit cost, a $19 plan can carry 100 round credits and still hold roughly a 55% gross margin after modeled payment fees and $2.50 of per-account overhead. That is still low by traditional SaaS standards, but defensible for an early, infrastructure-heavy product.

Implementation Changes

Plan constants

  • Rename Starter to Builder, or keep the code `starter` and show "Builder" publicly.
  • Update paid plan constants to $19 / 100, $79 / 500, $199 / 1,400, $499 / 3,600.
  • Set 100-credit top-up prices to $25 / $22 / $20 / $16 across Builder, Studio, Growth, and Business.
  • Keep Standard-only for Builder; allow Plus at Studio; advertise Max only after Daytona sandbox limits are raised.
  • Set per-run maximums to control blast radius: Builder 4 credits, Studio 12, Growth 24, Business 48.

Billing mechanics

  • Change `TopupUnitCredits` from 10 to 100 if extra-credit prices are sold as low-cost overage blocks.
  • Pin Standard sandbox resources to 2 vCPU / 6 GiB / 10 GiB on create.
  • Replace the old Pro/Max flavor ladder with Plus at 4 vCPU / 8 GiB / 10 GiB and defer Max until Daytona account limits are raised.
  • Keep auto-top-up monthly caps configurable as a billing safety setting, not as plan packaging.
  • Keep failed and cancelled runs billable once a sandbox starts.
  • Add automated archive enforcement for failed debug sandboxes after a grace period.

Decision

Ship the generous ladder if Hetchy remains BYO Claude/Codex credentials. Do not market it as "unlimited." Market it as "clear monthly cloud-agent capacity" with transparent credits, clear caps, and fair extra blocks. Before launch, reconcile the Standard resource tuple so Daytona creation and billing meters agree. If Hetchy later bundles Anthropic inference, add a separate model-usage meter or a much higher AI-included plan.
